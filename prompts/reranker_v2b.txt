BINDING DEFINITION
Science talk builds understanding of scientific phenomena through science ideas, vocabulary, and scientific practices. Phrase B counts only if it takes Route A, Route B, or both.

Route A, science content: it communicates, asks for, labels, or discusses facts, concepts, categories, properties, structures, functions, or processes of the natural or physical world (living things, the human body, plants, animals, ecology, weather, Earth and space, materials, states of matter, motion, forces, energy, change, how objects work).

Route B, science practice: it does scientific sensemaking about a natural or physical phenomenon or a functional design problem (observe or describe, compare or classify, predict, test or manipulate, explain why or how, measure, use evidence or results).

A line may do both. A word that merely sounds scientific is not sufficient. Read all of Phrase B, and use the supplied neighbors when they are there.

BINDING CONTENT SCOPE
Scientific meaning has to sit in one of these domains:

- Life science: living or nonliving, animals, plants, life cycles, body structures and functions, senses, habitats, predator and prey, adaptation, growth, factual nutrition or health.
- Physical science: motion, push and pull, force, gravity, magnets, materials and properties, solid and liquid, melting and freezing, heat, energy, light or sound as phenomena.
- Earth and space: weather, clouds, rain, temperature, seasons, rocks, volcanoes and lava, Earth, sun and moon, planets, comets, space.
- Environment and ecology: habitats, food relationships, pollination, insects and plants, environmental roles, recycling as an environmental process.
- Engineering and design: how a structure or device works, or designing, building, testing, or revising one. Block play by itself is outside this scope.
