OPERATIONAL DEFINITION OF SCIENCE TALK (binding)
Science talk is language that builds understanding of scientific phenomena through science ideas, vocabulary, and scientific practices. Phrase B is science talk only when it meets either or both of these routes:

A. Science content talk: communicates, requests, labels, or discusses information, facts, concepts, categories, properties, structures, functions, or processes about the natural or physical world. Typical targets include living things, the human body, plants, animals, ecology, weather, Earth and space, materials, states of matter, motion, forces, energy, change, and how objects work.

B. Science practice talk: engages in scientific sensemaking about a natural or physical phenomenon or a functional design problem. Practices include observing or describing, comparing or classifying, predicting, testing or manipulating, explaining why or how, measuring, and using evidence or results.

The two routes are not mutually exclusive. One utterance may contain both. Words that sound like science content alone are not enough. Judge the whole of Phrase B, using the supplied neighboring lines when they are present.

SCOPE OF SCIENCE CONTENT (binding)
Count Phrase B as science content only when it is genuinely conveying or eliciting scientific meaning inside one of these domains:

- Life science: living and nonliving; animals; plants; life cycles; body structures and functions; senses; habitats; predator and prey; adaptation; growth; nutrition or health when treated factually.
- Physical science: motion; push and pull; force; gravity; magnets; materials and properties; solid and liquid; melting and freezing; heat; energy; light or sound when treated as phenomena.
- Earth and space science: weather; clouds; rain; temperature; seasons; rocks; volcanoes and lava; Earth; sun and moon; planets; comets; space.
- Environmental and ecological science: habitats; food relationships; pollination; insects and plants; environmental roles; recycling when discussed as an environmental process.
- Engineering and design: how objects or structures work; designing, building, testing, or revising a functional structure or device. Children merely playing with blocks is not science.
